[30] (style=Normal) (3 words) 1.1 Project Context
[31] (style=Normal) (65 words) In the contemporary Philippine basic education system, Senior High School (SHS) educators carry an increasingly complex dual mandate: delivering rigorous, competency-based classroom instruction while simultaneously fulfilling extensive administrative and clerical reporting responsibilities. At Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School, an established public secondary institution located in Barangay Pansol, Calamba City, Laguna, teachers encounter significant operational friction in managing day-to-day classroom logistics across multiple large-enrollment class sections.
[32] (style=Normal) (129 words) The institutional client for this study, Ma’am Karen P. Gonzaga, is a Senior High School Mathematics Teacher at Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School tasked with instructional delivery across core and specialized mathematics offerings, including General Mathematics and Statistics and Probability for Grade 11 learners. Under current classroom workflows, daily student attendance is monitored predominantly through manual paper attendance sheets or physical index cards. In a typical class section of 40 to 50 students, conducting manual verbal roll calls or passing paper sheets expends approximately 10 to 15 minutes of the scheduled 60-minute instructional period. Beyond instructional time loss, paper-based records are highly vulnerable to physical deterioration, misplacement, and proxy signing, while compiling cumulative quarterly attendance tallies for institutional reporting requires labor-intensive manual counting across hundreds of individual paper entries.
[33] (style=Normal) (104 words) Parallel administrative bottlenecks compromise assessment and instructional resource management. Student submissions for written works and performance tasks are recorded across disparate paper logs, personal notebooks, and disconnected cloud storage folders, resulting in tracking delays and lack of centralized visibility. Furthermore, computing quarterly student grades under Department of Education (DepEd) Order No. 015, s. 2026 mandates multi-step mathematical calculations involving percentage contributions for Written Work, Performance Tasks, and Quarterly Assessments, followed by transmutation using an official 41-tier table. Executing these calculations manually on paper ledgers or standalone spreadsheets introduces recurrent mathematical transcription errors and delays the encoding of official DepEd Electronic Class Records (E-Class Records).
[34] (style=Normal) (94 words) These classroom operational delays are further exacerbated by pedagogical preparation burdens. Teachers expend substantial non-teaching hours drafting weekly lesson plans and organizing fragmented instructional materials—such as curriculum guides, lesson exemplars, slide decks, and video resources—scattered across personal flash drives and varied online repositories. Simultaneously, communication with parents remains largely episodic, occurring primarily during end-of-quarter parent-teacher conferences, which deprives families of timely visibility into chronic student absenteeism or slipping academic performance. Addressing these interconnected logistical challenges necessitates an integrated, multi-platform educational management system tailored specifically to the operational realities of Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School.
[35] (style=Normal) (4 words) 1.2 Purpose and Description
[36] (style=Normal) (64 words) The primary purpose of this capstone research is to design, develop, and implement CLASSREALM (Comprehensive Learning and Academic Student Supervision Realm), an integrated Senior High School management platform engineered to automate and unify attendance tracking, activity and requirement submissions, objective assessment grading, DepEd DO 15, s. 2026 grade computation, teacher lesson planning guide generation, and instructional materials management for Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School.
[37] (style=Normal) (19 words) CLASSREALM is architected as an offline-resilient, multi-platform ecosystem comprising four specialized user portals designed to serve distinct institutional stakeholders:
[38] (style=Normal) (41 words) 1. Admin Portal (Web): A restricted web interface assigned exclusively to the client teacher for high-level administrative maintenance, specifically scoped to provisioning and activating teacher accounts and executing annual academic year rollovers, without access to classroom instructional data or student grades.
[39] (style=Normal) (51 words) 2. Teacher Portal (Responsive Web App): A comprehensive web-based workspace for teachers to self-manage class sections and subjects, plot class schedules, record attendance via RFID scanner tap or permanent manual roster entry, administer quizzes, execute automated DepEd DO 15 grade calculations, generate structured lesson plan guides, and manage digital learning resources.
[40] (style=Normal) (32 words) 3. Student Portal (Mobile App): A dedicated cross-platform mobile application providing authenticated learners with secure, view-only access to their personal attendance history, submitted requirement grades, objective online quizzes, and downloadable instructional files.
[41] (style=Normal) (34 words) 4. Parent Portal (Mobile App): A dedicated view-only mobile application enabling verified parents and legal guardians to monitor their linked child's real-time attendance status, unexcused absence warnings, pending requirement deadlines, and quarterly academic performance.
[42] (style=Normal) (109 words) To ensure operational continuity in public school environments subject to fluctuating electrical power and intermittent classroom internet connectivity, CLASSREALM incorporates a dual-path attendance ingestion pipeline. The primary attendance mechanism consists of a standalone Internet of Things (IoT) hardware scanner powered by an ESP32 microcontroller and an RC522/PN532 Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) reader. The device operates independently with local non-volatile flash storage (SPIFFS/NVS), queuing student card scans during network blackouts and automatically synchronizing logs to the Supabase cloud backend upon reconnection. In addition, manual attendance logging via the digital class roster is permanently maintained as a non-negotiable operational fallback, ensuring that zero student logs are lost under any operational condition.
[43] (style=Normal) (85 words) Beyond attendance, CLASSREALM unifies academic assessment and pedagogical support. The platform provides automated checking of objective quiz items, instant psychometric item analysis, mathematical validation of grading weights to an exact 100% total, automated 41-tier DepEd transmutation, and standardized grade sheet exports (PDF, CSV, Excel). For instructional preparation, the system integrates a Lesson Plan Guide Generator that structures DepEd-aligned lesson plans for Grade 11 Mathematics, alongside a centralized Learning Materials Organizer that provides role-governed file storage and retrieval, establishing a unified digital realm for classroom management.

[47] (style=Normal) (3 words) 1.3.2 Specific Objectives

[50] (style=Normal) (68 words) 2. Schedule-Bound Auto-Status & Cutoff Engine: To implement an automated attendance evaluation engine bound strictly to plotted subject class schedules, enforcing teacher-configured chronological cutoffs (Present window, Late cutoff, Absent cutoff) and executing an automated serverless backend job (Supabase Edge Function) at 23:59 each school day that marks unscanned enrolled students as Absent without overwriting existing valid marks (Present, Late, Excused) or triggering on non-scheduled days or official holidays.
[51] (style=Normal) (45 words) 3. Activity and Requirement Tracking Module: To construct a requirement tracking module supporting teacher assignment creation with attachments and student electronic file submissions, enforcing submission deadline verification within ±1.0 second and delivering real-time push notification alerts to student and parent mobile applications within 3.0 seconds.
[52] (style=Normal) (48 words) 4. Objective Assessment & Item Analysis Engine: To implement an online quiz and examination engine supporting objective item formats (multiple choice, true/false, identification) with automated score evaluation completed within 1.0 second upon submission, and automated calculation of psychometric item analysis (difficulty index and discrimination index) within 2.0 seconds.
[53] (style=Normal) (72 words) 5. DepEd DO 15, s. 2026 Grading Engine: To engineer an automated grading computation engine adhering strictly to DepEd Order No. 015, s. 2026 for Senior High School subjects (e.g., General Mathematics: Written Work 20%, Performance Tasks 50%, Quarterly Assessment 30%), validating that teacher component percentages total exactly 100%, computing initial weighted scores rounded to 2 decimal places, and mapping final ratings to the official 41-tier transmutation scale with 100% mathematical accuracy.
[54] (style=Normal) (49 words) 6. Teacher Lesson Plan Guide Generator: To design an interactive lesson plan guide generator that scaffolds DepEd-aligned lesson plans (objectives, content standards, learning competencies, instructional procedures, and assessment alignment) specifically for Grade 11 Mathematics instruction, reducing manual lesson drafting time by at least 50% while preserving teacher editorial control.
[55] (style=Normal) (39 words) 7. [56] (style=Normal) (47 words) 8. Role-Governed Portals & Row-Level Security: To implement dedicated Student and Parent mobile portals enforcing Supabase Row-Level Security (RLS) policies, ensuring learners view only their personal academic history and parents access strictly their linked child's attendance, absence warnings (>=3 absences), and quarterly grades with 100% data isolation.
[57] (style=Normal) (37 words) 9. [59] (style=Normal) (7 words) 1.4 Scope and Limitations of the Study
[60] (style=Normal) (5 words) 1.4.1 Scope of the Study
[61] (style=Normal) (111 words) The scope of this research is bounded by the operational, technical, and pedagogical requirements of its pilot implementation at Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School in Barangay Pansol, Calamba City, Laguna, during School Year 2026–2027. The primary client is Ma’am Karen P. Gonzaga, Senior High School Mathematics Teacher, handling approximately 60 to 70 students across three class sections. The primary beneficiaries are Grade 11 students enrolled in core mathematics subjects (General Mathematics, Statistics and Probability), their parents or legal guardians, and the client teacher. While CLASSREALM is initially deployed for a single teacher, its role-based architecture is designed to support additional teachers should the institution expand its adoption in future school years.
[62] (style=Normal) (7 words) The software scope encompasses four interconnected portals:
[63] (style=Normal) (14 words) • Admin Portal (Web): Enables academic year setup, rollover transitions, and teacher credential provisioning.
[64] (style=Normal) (55 words) • Teacher Portal (Responsive Web App): Empowers the teacher to self-manage class sections and student enrollments, configure plotted class schedules, capture attendance via RFID scanner or permanent manual roster logging, publish requirements, administer objective online quizzes, compute transmutated quarterly grades under DepEd DO 15, s. 2026, generate structured lesson plan guides, and manage instructional materials.
[65] (style=Normal) (24 words) • Student Portal (Mobile App): Provides authenticated students with personal views of daily attendance logs, submitted assignment grades, quiz scores, and downloadable learning resources.
[66] (style=Normal) (32 words) • Parent Portal (Mobile App): Provides authorized parents with linked view-only monitoring of daily attendance timestamps, immediate absence alert notifications for 3 or more absences, pending academic requirements, and official quarterly grades.
[67] (style=Normal) (83 words) The technical hardware scope includes a standalone ESP32 DevKit microcontroller interfaced with an MFRC522/PN532 RFID reader, status LEDs, a piezo buzzer, and a rechargeable battery circuit, capable of non-volatile offline scan buffering. The software architecture utilizes Flutter for client applications, Supabase PostgreSQL with Row-Level Security for relational data persistence, and Firebase Authentication for secure identity governance. Furthermore, the system preserves academic and grade records across multiple school years and allows downloading and exporting consolidated class records into PDF, CSV, and Microsoft Excel formats.
[68] (style=Normal) (4 words) 1.4.2 Boundaries and Limitations
[69] (style=Normal) (19 words) To maintain structural integrity, data security, and institutional compliance, the study establishes the following explicit boundaries and functional limitations:
[70] (style=Normal) (56 words) 1. System Admin Boundaries & Separation of Duties: The System Admin account is assigned to the client teacher solely for academic year transitions and teacher provisioning. To preserve instructional autonomy and protect student confidentiality, the Admin CANNOT manage sections, subjects, schedules, attendance, activities, requirements, quizzes, examinations, grading percentages, lesson plans, learning materials, or student academic records.
[71] (style=Normal) (30 words) 2. Teacher Roster Self-Management: Teachers self-manage sections, subjects, and students after receiving class rosters from the registrar/school. The software empowers educators to maintain their own class sections without administrative intervention.
[72] (style=Normal) (30 words) 3. Excluded Institutional Roles: The system strictly excludes Registrar and Principal user accounts, roles, and administrative governance dashboards. School-wide enrollment reporting and principal administrative supervision remain outside the software scope.
[73] (style=Normal) (56 words) 4. Excluded Student Workflows: The system does not support or automate mid-year student transfers, cross-section switching, or formal student dropping procedures. Under DepEd regulations, student dropping requires formal home visitation, parent-teacher conferences, and guidance counseling documentation; consequently, dropping or transferring students must be resolved through official school administrative channels and reflected manually in the teacher's roster.
[74] (style=Normal) (43 words) 5. Permanent Manual Attendance Fallback: Manual attendance marking directly on the teacher's roster is a permanent, non-negotiable operational feature. It is not an emergency workaround but an essential operational mode enabling teachers to accommodate forgotten RFID cards, temporary hardware malfunctions, or school activities.
[75] (style=Normal) (27 words) 6. Assessment Network Dependency: Online quiz and examination administration requires an active internet connection. Offline test administration is excluded due to real-time answer verification and security requirements.
[76] (style=Normal) (33 words) 7. Automated Scoring Boundaries: Automated assessment scoring is strictly restricted to supported objective question types (multiple choice, true/false, and short identification). Subjective problem-solving, open essays, and multi-step mathematical proofs require manual teacher evaluation.
[77] (style=Normal) (47 words) 8. Anti-Cheat Monitoring Boundaries: The application incorporates client-side assessment session event logging (detecting app focus loss, screen switching, or browser tab blur) to generate infraction flags for teacher review. However, the software cannot prevent students from consulting external physical notes or secondary unmonitored devices during remote testing.
[78] (style=Normal) (40 words) 9. Telephony & Carrier Limitations: Push notifications and in-app alerts are delivered via Firebase Cloud Messaging. Automated cellular SMS notifications depend on external hardware GSM modems or third-party SMS API aggregators, which require active carrier signal coverage and account credits.

[80] (style=Normal) (24 words) To verify the successful deployment and empirical acceptability of CLASSREALM during School Year 2026–2027, the system is governed by the following quantifiable performance benchmarks:
[81] (style=Normal) (23 words) • Attendance Tap Latency: RFID card scan detection and audiovisual feedback (LED/buzzer) executed within < 2.0 seconds under the standalone ESP32 hardware scanner.
[82] (style=Normal) (29 words) • Offline Data Buffer Capacity: The ESP32 standalone scanner retains at least 5,000 attendance records in local non-volatile flash memory without data loss during extended power or Wi-Fi outages.
[83] (style=Normal) (22 words) • Duplicate Scan Suppression: 100% idempotent suppression of duplicate card taps within the same class session, preventing timestamp overwrites or status downgrades.
[84] (style=Normal) (20 words) • Cloud Synchronization Latency: Locally buffered offline attendance logs synchronize completely to Supabase within < 5.0 seconds upon network restoration.
[85] (style=Normal) (25 words) • Assessment Turnaround: Objective quiz scoring completed in < 1.0 second per submission; psychometric item analysis (difficulty and discrimination indices) computed within < 2.0 seconds.
[86] (style=Normal) (37 words) • Grading Mathematical Precision: 100% adherence to DepEd Order No. 015, s. 2026 component weighting, with running initial grades rounded to 2 decimal places and final ratings transmutated via the official 41-tier table with zero computational error.
[87] (style=Normal) (19 words) • Notification Dispatch Speed: Real-time push notifications for student absence warnings and requirement announcements delivered within < 3.0 seconds.
[88] (style=Normal) (29 words) • ISO/IEC 25010:2023 Technical Quality: Attainment of an overall weighted mean score of >= 4.20 out of 5.00 ('Highly Acceptable') across all nine software quality characteristics during stakeholder evaluation.
[89] (style=Normal) (4 words) 1.6 Definition of Terms

[91] (style=Normal) (55 words) 1. ESP32: A low-cost, low-power 32-bit dual-core microcontroller equipped with integrated 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth connectivity, utilized in CLASSREALM as a standalone edge scanner that interfaces with an RFID module to capture student card UIDs, validate timestamps, buffer scans in non-volatile flash memory during network outages, and synchronize attendance logs to the cloud backend.
[92] (style=Normal) (47 words) 2. RFID (Radio Frequency Identification): A contactless wireless communication technology operating via electromagnetic fields (specifically 13.56 MHz high frequency or 125 kHz low frequency) used in CLASSREALM to detect unique card identification codes (UIDs) mapped to student 12-digit Learner Reference Numbers (LRNs) for rapid classroom attendance logging.
[93] (style=Normal) (38 words) 3. Firebase Authentication: A cloud-based identity and access management service integrated into CLASSREALM to manage secure user credential validation, encrypted password storage, JSON Web Token (JWT) issuance, and first-time temporary password resets for administrators, teachers, students, and parents.
[94] (style=Normal) (32 words) 4. Supabase: An open-source Backend-as-a-Service (BaaS) built on PostgreSQL, serving as CLASSREALM’s primary relational database for persistent academic records, real-time data replication, automated scheduled cron jobs (Edge Functions), and backend security enforcement.
[95] (style=Normal) (43 words) 5.
