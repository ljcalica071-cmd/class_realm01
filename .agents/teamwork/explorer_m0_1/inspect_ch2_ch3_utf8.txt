[CH2 P103][6w] 2.1 Thematic Review of Related Literature
[CH2 P104][99w] The evolution of modern educational management systems represents a necessary convergence between administrative automation, classroom instructional supervision, and stakeholder transparency. Senior High School educators in the Philippines face severe administrative workloads encompassing manual roll calls, requirement tracking, grade computation, lesson plan drafting, and learning resource management. CLASSREALM addresses these challenges through four core integrated modules: (1) automated attendance tracking with manual fallback, (2) automated grading and assessment computation, (3) teacher lesson planning guide generation, and (4) centralized learning materials organization. The following literature review analyzes empirical studies published between 2024 and 2026 that substantiate these technical and operational domains.
[CH2 P105][7w] 2.1.1 Automated and Dual-Path Attendance Tracking Systems
[CH2 P106][21w] Automated attendance tracking significantly reduces the instructional time lost during traditional pen-and-paper roll calls while mitigating data falsification and recording errors.
[CH2 P107][113w] In a local Philippine empirical study, Holgado et al. (2026) developed and evaluated an automated attendance monitoring system utilizing Ultra High Frequency (UHF) RFID and a Raspberry Pi microcomputer for the Senior High School department of Colegio de San Juan de Letran Manila. Utilizing a descriptive-developmental research design, the researchers executed physical walk-through detection trials and conducted faculty usability surveys. The empirical findings demonstrated a 93.3% RFID tag detection accuracy during walk-through transit and a 100% success rate in backend database logging. Faculty evaluators registered an overall mean score within the 'Strongly Agree' band for software usability and hardware reliability, affirming that contactless radio-frequency identification substantially eliminates classroom queuing and administrative roll-call delays.
[CH2 P108][94w] Complementing automated hardware capture, Navarra and Antonio (2025) evaluated a multi-stakeholder student monitoring and data tracking system deployed at Isulan National High School, Philippines. Developed using an Agile software development lifecycle spanning seven sprints and evaluated through a descriptive-evaluative survey with N = 85 respondents (comprising teachers, parents, and secondary students), the system achieved an overall grand mean rating of 4.72 ± 0.43 (interpreted as 'Very Functional and Highly Effective'). Crucially, 95% of respondents confirmed that digitizing attendance logs and academic records eliminated manual record reconciliation while establishing cross-stakeholder transparency across parents and faculty.

[CH2 P131][3w] 2.3 Theoretical Framework
[CH2 P132][85w] The theoretical foundation of this study is grounded in General Systems Theory and operationalized through the Input-Process-Storage-Output (IPSO) architectural paradigm, substantiated by the Information Systems Success Model formulated by DeLone and McLean (2003). In modern educational data management, an academic tracking and attendance platform cannot be structured as an ephemeral transactional utility that transforms and discards data within a single user session; rather, it represents a persistent cybernetic system in which data must persist, be retrieved, and be audited over time across multi-year academic cohorts.
[CH2 P133][80w] While the updated DeLone and McLean (2003) model articulates the causal dimensions of information system success—comprising System Quality, Information Quality, Service Quality, System Use, User Satisfaction, and Net Benefits—the operational flow of academic records through CLASSREALM is structured around the sequential and interdependent stages of the IPSO paradigm. As conceptualized, raw administrative and instructional inputs are systematically captured, processed through specialized validation and computation algorithms, persisted within secure cloud storage and non-volatile hardware buffers, and rendered into verified academic deliverables.
[CH2 P134][2w] Figure 1
[CH2 P135][5w] Input-Process-Storage-Output Diagram (IPSO) of CLASSREALM
[CH2 P137][110w] Figure 1 illustrates the flow of data through CLASSREALM: official student rosters, RFID card taps, plotted class schedules, assessment scores, grading criteria, and curriculum competencies are captured at the Input stage; validated, scored, computed, and synchronized at the Process stage; persisted in a role-protected Supabase database, secure file storage, and encrypted audit logs at the Storage stage; and rendered as audit-defensible attendance logs, DepEd E-Class Records, absence warning alerts, lesson plan guides, and multi-portal dashboards at the Output stage. A deficiency observed at the Output stage (for example, an inaccurate transmutated grade, duplicate attendance timestamp, or unreflected submission) can be traced backward through Storage and Process to its Input-stage origin.
[CH2 P138][12w] The four interconnected stages of the IPSO theoretical architecture function as follows:
[CH2 P139][80w] 1. Input: Encompasses the foundational administrative, physical, and instructional data ingested into the system. This includes official registrar-provided class rosters with 12-digit Learner Reference Numbers (LRNs), physical RFID card UIDs (captured via the standalone ESP32 hardware scanner or permanent manual roster entry), plotted class timetables with teacher cutoff thresholds (Present, Late, Absent), assignment requirements, quiz item banks, raw assessment scores across Written Work, Performance Tasks, and Quarterly Exams, DepEd Order No. 015, s. 2026 grading percentages, and curriculum learning competencies.
[CH2 P140][85w] 2. Process: Represents the computational, algorithmic, and serverless workflows executed upon the ingested inputs. Core processes comprise role-based user authentication and session management via Firebase Authentication, schedule-bound attendance filtering that suppresses duplicate card taps and rejects scans outside class periods, ESP32 non-volatile offline scan buffering and cloud synchronization, a nightly 23:59 Supabase Edge Function auto-marking unscanned students as Absent, real-time objective quiz checking and psychometric item analysis, DepEd DO 15 weighted grade computation and 41-tier transmutation, structured lesson plan guide generation, and push notification dispatch.
[CH2 P141][60w] 3. Storage: Maintains the secure, relational persistence and multi-year archival of all educational data artifacts. This stage is hosted on an enterprise Supabase PostgreSQL relational database enforced by Row-Level Security (RLS) policies by user role, Supabase Storage buckets for digital learning materials and student electronic file submissions, and immutable audit logs that preserve historical records across successive academic school years.
[CH2 P142][72w] 4. Output: Delivers the actionable, verified educational deliverables to institutional stakeholders. Key outputs encompass audit-defensible attendance logs explicitly tagged with 'RFID' or 'Manual' capture indicators, real-time student absence alerts for 3 or more absences dispatched to parents and teachers, official DepEd E-Class Records exportable to PDF, Microsoft Excel, and CSV formats, structured Grade 11 Mathematics lesson plan guides, a searchable digital learning materials repository, and view-only personal dashboards for students and parents.
[CH2 P143][93w] This IPSO theoretical architecture directly operationalizes the Information Systems Success dimensions articulated by DeLone and McLean (2003). High System Quality (sub-second edge RFID scanning, local buffering during power interruptions, and database reliability) and high Service Quality (Firebase role-based security, Row-Level Security data segregation, and inline form validation) jointly govern the Process and Storage stages. In turn, these drive high Information Quality (mathematically verified DepEd grade transmutation and tamper-resistant attendance records) at the Output stage, maximizing user satisfaction among teachers, students, and parents and generating institutional net benefits for Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School.

[CH2 P206][4w] 3.4 Data Collection Procedure
[CH2 P207][25w] The empirical data collection for CLASSREALM was executed in six systematic, chronological stages designed to protect participant confidentiality while ensuring rigorous technical and operational verification:
[CH2 P208][71w] Stage 1: Institutional Clearance and Ethical Endorsement. Formal written communication was addressed to the Office of the Principal at Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School and the instructional client, Ma’am Karen P. Gonzaga, requesting institutional authorization to pilot the CLASSREALM platform within Grade 11 Mathematics classes. Parental consent forms and student assent forms were distributed to legal guardians, explicitly outlining voluntary participation, data encryption protocols, and unconditional withdrawal rights without academic prejudice.
[CH2 P209][52w] Stage 2: Requirements Elicitation and Document Review. The researchers conducted structured technical interviews with Ma’am Karen P. Gonzaga to document current operational friction. Physical artifacts—including manual attendance notebooks, index cards, and quarterly grading ledgers—were audited to extract authentic data fields, schedule windows, and calculation rules under DepEd Order No. 015, s. 2026.
[CH2 P210][58w] Stage 3: Research Instrument Validation and Expert Review. The survey instruments—adapted from the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 product quality standard—were submitted to a panel of three faculty experts in computing and educational research for content validity review. Items were adjusted to ensure role-appropriate language, ensuring students and parents rated observable functional usability while technical evaluators inspected security, architecture, and maintainability.
[CH2 P211][67w] Stage 4: Hardware Stationing and Controlled Classroom Trials. The standalone ESP32 edge scanner was installed in a robust acrylic enclosure at the primary entrance of the Grade 11 classroom. Students were issued passive 13.56 MHz RFID cards mapped to their 12-digit Learner Reference Numbers (LRNs). Controlled walk-through tapping sessions were conducted to capture baseline detection latency, duplicate tap suppression, and offline buffering performance under deliberate Wi-Fi disconnection.
[CH2 P212][57w] Stage 5: Live Pilot Implementation and Academic Workflow Execution. Over a three-week trial window, CLASSREALM was utilized for daily attendance recording, objective quiz administration, requirement submissions, automated grade computation, and iLAW lesson plan generation. The nightly 23:59 Supabase Edge Function was monitored to verify automated absent marking, while parent mobile push alerts were audited for dispatch timeliness.
[CH2 P213][72w] Stage 6: Post-Deployment Acceptability Survey and Encrypted Data Archival. Following the pilot period, the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 evaluation questionnaires were administered to the client teacher, students, parents, and IT experts. Collected ratings were coded, verified, and analyzed. In strict compliance with Republic Act No. 10173 (Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012), all personal identifiers were sanitized, and raw records were archived in an AES-256 encrypted database repository accessible solely to authorized project researchers.
[CH2 P214][3w] 3.5 Requirement Analysis
[CH2 P215][70w] The requirement analysis established that Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School operates under severe logistical constraints: manual attendance taking consumes 10 to 15 minutes of every 60-minute period; paper records are prone to damage and proxy signing; computing transmutated grades under DepEd DO 15, s. 2026 requires multi-step manual calculations susceptible to transcription error; lesson planning documentation creates substantial administrative fatigue; and parent notifications are delayed until quarterly report card distribution.
[CH2 P216][16w] To resolve these operational bottlenecks, CLASSREALM defines four authenticated user roles operating across dedicated interface platforms:
[CH2 P217][30w] • System Admin (Restricted Web): Assigned to the client teacher solely for annual academic year rollovers and teacher account provisioning, with zero visibility into classroom instructional data or student grades.
[CH2 P218][42w] • Teacher (Responsive Web App): Full instructional management for plotting class schedules, configuring schedule cutoffs (Present, Late, Absent), monitoring RFID taps, executing permanent manual roster overrides, computing DO 15 transmutated grades, generating lesson plan guides via iLAW, and distributing digital learning files.
[CH2 P219][26w] • Student (Dedicated Mobile App): View-only personal attendance timestamps, objective online quiz answering, electronic assignment file uploading, and viewing transmutated quarterly grades under strict Row-Level Security.
[CH2 P220][32w] • Parent / Guardian (Dedicated Mobile App): Real-time visibility into the linked child's attendance status, instant push alerts for 3 or more cumulative unexcused absences, requirement deadlines, and official quarterly academic ratings.

[CH2 P234][8w] 3.8 Design of Software, Systems, Product and/or Processes
[CH2 P235][68w] CLASSREALM is architected as an offline-resilient, multi-platform client-cloud educational ecosystem. [CH2 P252][4w] 3.10 Description of Prototype
[CH2 P253][27w] CLASSREALM exists as a fully functioning software prototype operating across web and mobile platforms with integrated edge IoT hardware. The prototype encompasses four distinct, role-governed user portals:
[CH2 P254][59w] 1. Admin Web Portal (Working): Accessible exclusively to the client teacher through secure Firebase authentication. Provides dedicated interfaces for provisioning new teacher credentials, managing institutional user lifecycles, and initiating academic year transitions (e.g., advancing from School Year 2026–2027 to 2027–2028). The admin portal strictly enforces separation of duties with zero access to classroom rosters, instructional materials, or student grades.
[CH2 P255][113w] 2. Teacher Responsive Web Portal (Working): The primary operational workspace for Ma’am Karen P. Gonzaga. Features include: (a) Class Section and Student Management for enrolling students with 12-digit LRNs and mapping RFID card UIDs; (b) Schedule Configuration for defining class meeting times and lateness thresholds; (c) Live Attendance Monitoring displaying real-time RFID tap timestamps alongside a permanent manual roster toggle for recording excused absences or accommodating forgotten cards; (d) DepEd DO 15 Grade Computation Calculator providing automatic 41-tier transmutation and one-click E-Class Record export to PDF and Excel; (e) iLAW Lesson Plan Guide Generator scaffolding Grade 11 Mathematics lesson exemplars; and (f) Centralized Learning Materials Organizer for uploading curriculum modules and slide presentations.
[CH2 P256][49w] 3. Student Mobile Portal (Working): Developed in Flutter for Android devices. Authenticated learners access personal academic dashboards displaying daily attendance status (Present, Late, Absent, Excused), submitted assignment grades, online objective quizzes with immediate score feedback, and downloadable teacher learning materials. Row-Level Security ensures students view strictly their personal records.
[CH2 P257][46w] 4. Parent Mobile Portal (Working): Developed in Flutter for parent and guardian monitoring. Authorized guardians view real-time arrival and departure timestamps for their linked child, track academic assignment completion, receive instant push notifications upon accumulating three or more unexcused absences, and view official transmutated quarterly grades.
