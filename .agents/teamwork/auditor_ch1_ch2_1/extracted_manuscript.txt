[030 | Normal]: 1.1 Project Context
[031 | Normal]: In the contemporary Philippine basic education system, Senior High School (SHS) educators carry an increasingly complex dual mandate: delivering rigorous, competency-based classroom instruction while simultaneously fulfilling extensive administrative and clerical reporting responsibilities. At Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School, an established public secondary institution located in Barangay Pansol, Calamba City, Laguna, teachers encounter significant operational friction in managing day-to-day classroom logistics across multiple large-enrollment class sections.
[032 | Normal]: The institutional client for this study, Ma’am Karen P. Gonzaga, is a Senior High School Mathematics Teacher at Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School tasked with instructional delivery across core and specialized mathematics offerings, including General Mathematics and Statistics and Probability for Grade 11 learners. Under current classroom workflows, daily student attendance is monitored predominantly through manual paper attendance sheets or physical index cards. In a typical class section of 40 to 50 students, conducting manual verbal roll calls or passing paper sheets expends approximately 10 to 15 minutes of the scheduled 60-minute instructional period. Beyond instructional time loss, paper-based records are highly vulnerable to physical deterioration, misplacement, and proxy signing, while compiling cumulative quarterly attendance tallies for institutional reporting requires labor-intensive manual counting across hundreds of individual paper entries.
[033 | Normal]: Parallel administrative bottlenecks compromise assessment and instructional resource management. Student submissions for written works and performance tasks are recorded across disparate paper logs, personal notebooks, and disconnected cloud storage folders, resulting in tracking delays and lack of centralized visibility. Furthermore, computing quarterly student grades under Department of Education (DepEd) Order No. 015, s. 2026 mandates multi-step mathematical calculations involving percentage contributions for Written Work, Performance Tasks, and Quarterly Assessments, followed by transmutation using an official 41-tier table. Executing these calculations manually on paper ledgers or standalone spreadsheets introduces recurrent mathematical transcription errors and delays the encoding of official DepEd Electronic Class Records (E-Class Records).
[034 | Normal]: These classroom operational delays are further exacerbated by pedagogical preparation burdens. Teachers expend substantial non-teaching hours drafting weekly lesson plans and organizing fragmented instructional materials—such as curriculum guides, lesson exemplars, slide decks, and video resources—scattered across personal flash drives and varied online repositories. Simultaneously, communication with parents remains largely episodic, occurring primarily during end-of-quarter parent-teacher conferences, which deprives families of timely visibility into chronic student absenteeism or slipping academic performance. Addressing these interconnected logistical challenges necessitates an integrated, multi-platform educational management system tailored specifically to the operational realities of Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School.
[035 | Normal]: 1.2 Purpose and Description
[036 | Normal]: The primary purpose of this capstone research is to design, develop, and implement CLASSREALM (Comprehensive Learning and Academic Student Supervision Realm), an integrated Senior High School management platform engineered to automate and unify attendance tracking, activity and requirement submissions, objective assessment grading, DepEd DO 15, s. 2026 grade computation, teacher lesson planning guide generation, and instructional materials management for Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School.
[037 | Normal]: CLASSREALM is architected as an offline-resilient, multi-platform ecosystem comprising four specialized user portals designed to serve distinct institutional stakeholders:
[038 | Normal]: 1. Admin Portal (Restricted Web Module): A restricted web interface assigned exclusively to the client teacher for high-level administrative maintenance, specifically scoped to provisioning and activating teacher accounts and executing annual academic year rollovers, without access to classroom instructional data or student grades.
[039 | Normal]: 2. Teacher Portal (Responsive Web and App): A comprehensive workspace for teachers to self-manage class sections and subjects, plot class schedules, record attendance via dual-path RFID or permanent manual roster entry, administer quizzes, execute automated DepEd DO 15 grade calculations, generate structured lesson plan guides, and manage digital learning resources.
[040 | Normal]: 3. Student Portal (Dedicated Mobile App): A dedicated cross-platform mobile application providing authenticated learners with secure, view-only access to their personal attendance history, submitted requirement grades, objective online quizzes, and downloadable instructional files.
[041 | Normal]: 4. Parent Portal (Dedicated Mobile App): A dedicated view-only mobile interface enabling verified parents and legal guardians to monitor their linked child's real-time attendance status, unexcused absence warnings, pending requirement deadlines, and quarterly academic performance.
[042 | Normal]: To ensure operational continuity in public school environments subject to fluctuating electrical power and intermittent classroom internet connectivity, CLASSREALM incorporates a dual-path attendance ingestion pipeline. The primary attendance mechanism consists of a standalone Internet of Things (IoT) hardware scanner powered by an ESP32 microcontroller and an RC522/PN532 Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) reader. The device operates independently with local non-volatile flash storage (SPIFFS/NVS), queuing student card scans during network blackouts and automatically synchronizing logs to the Supabase cloud backend upon reconnection. As an immediate operational backup, teachers can utilize mobile smartphone Near Field Communication (NFC) scanning. In addition, manual attendance logging via the digital class roster is permanently maintained as a non-negotiable operational fallback, ensuring that zero student logs are lost under any operational condition.
[043 | Normal]: Beyond attendance, CLASSREALM unifies academic assessment and pedagogical support. The platform provides automated checking of objective quiz items, instant psychometric item analysis, mathematical validation of grading weights to an exact 100% total, automated 41-tier DepEd transmutation, and standardized grade sheet exports (PDF, CSV, Excel). For instructional preparation, the system integrates a Lesson Plan Guide Generator that structures DepEd-aligned lesson plans for Grade 11 Mathematics, alongside a centralized Learning Materials Organizer that provides role-governed file storage and retrieval, establishing a unified digital realm for classroom management.

[103 | Normal]: 2.1 Thematic Review of Related Literature
[104 | Normal]: The evolution of modern educational management systems represents a necessary convergence between administrative automation, classroom instructional supervision, and stakeholder transparency. Senior High School educators in the Philippines face severe administrative workloads encompassing manual roll calls, requirement tracking, grade computation, lesson plan drafting, and learning resource management. CLASSREALM addresses these challenges through four core integrated modules: (1) automated attendance tracking with manual fallback, (2) automated grading and assessment computation, (3) teacher lesson planning guide generation, and (4) centralized learning materials organization. The following literature review analyzes empirical studies published between 2021 and 2026 that substantiate these technical and operational domains.
[105 | Normal]: 2.1.1 Automated and Dual-Path Attendance Tracking Systems
[106 | Normal]: Automated attendance tracking significantly reduces the instructional time lost during traditional pen-and-paper roll calls while mitigating data falsification and recording errors.
[107 | Normal]: In a local Philippine empirical study, Holgado et al. (2026) developed and evaluated an automated attendance monitoring system utilizing Ultra High Frequency (UHF) RFID and a Raspberry Pi microcomputer for the Senior High School department of Colegio de San Juan de Letran Manila. Utilizing a descriptive-developmental research design, the researchers executed physical walk-through detection trials and conducted faculty usability surveys. The empirical findings demonstrated a 93.3% RFID tag detection accuracy during walk-through transit and a 100% success rate in backend database logging. Faculty evaluators registered an overall mean score within the 'Strongly Agree' band for software usability and hardware reliability, affirming that contactless radio-frequency identification substantially eliminates classroom queuing and administrative roll-call delays.
[108 | Normal]: Complementing automated hardware capture, Navarra and Antonio (2025) evaluated a multi-stakeholder student monitoring and data tracking system deployed at Isulan National High School, Philippines. Developed using an Agile software development lifecycle spanning seven sprints and evaluated through a descriptive-evaluative survey with N = 85 respondents (comprising teachers, parents, and secondary students), the system achieved an overall grand mean rating of 4.72 ± 0.43 (interpreted as 'Very Functional and Highly Effective'). Crucially, 95% of respondents confirmed that digitizing attendance logs and academic records eliminated manual record reconciliation while establishing cross-stakeholder transparency across parents and faculty.
[109 | Normal]: In the international domain, Irhamna, Kasmini, and Khairuman (2026) developed an IoT-based student attendance system integrating an ESP32 microcontroller, RC522 RFID reader module, and local MySQL database recording at Universitas Bina Bangsa Getsempena, Indonesia. Utilizing a Research and Development (R&D) prototyping methodology evaluated by technical experts and N = 35 student participants, empirical benchmark tests demonstrated an average card-reading and logging latency of only 0.10 seconds per scan. Expert content validity evaluated via Aiken's V formula yielded an index of 0.982 (interpreted as highly valid), with 100% data transmission reliability under local buffering, confirming the viability of low-cost ESP32 edge microcontrollers for rapid, offline-capable attendance recording.
[110 | Normal]: Further examining mobile smart attendance environments, Jantan, Mohd Zainudin, and Mustafa@Dain (2023) investigated the functionality and usability of a mobile smart attendance application operating via Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) beacon tracking at Universiti Poly-Tech Malaysia. Utilizing an iterative-incremental Agile methodology and evaluating system adoption among N = 140 participants (lecturers and students), quantitative survey results revealed that 87.9% of respondents strongly agreed that the automated mobile tracking application successfully assisted educators in monitoring absenteeism, enforcing schedule adherence, and eliminating instructional interruptions.
[111 | Normal]: 2.1.2 Automated Grading, Assessment Computation, and Academic Analytics
[112 | Normal]: Automating classroom grading and objective assessment workflows eliminates teacher computation fatigue, enforces mathematical accuracy, and provides timely diagnostic insights into student mastery.
[113 | Normal]: In the Philippine local context, Mahinay and Encarnacion (2024) developed and empirically tested an Online Student Monitoring and Evaluation System integrating the Apriori association-rule data mining algorithm at Saint Michael College of Caraga. Utilizing a descriptive-developmental and evaluative design with N = 83 respondents (n = 75 students and n = 8 faculty members), the empirical results indicated an overall weighted mean perception score of 4.16 (interpreted as 'Agree'). The specific indicator for evaluation calculation effectiveness scored 4.24, while feedback mechanisms scored 4.12. The study confirmed that automating student performance computations and assessment data aggregation significantly reduces grade submission turnaround times and equips teachers with predictive patterns regarding at-risk learners.
[114 | Normal]: In an international study examining digital assessment telemetry, Jones (2022) conducted an empirical investigation published in Frontiers in Education on N = 31 students to determine the quantitative relationships between online attendance, digital engagement metrics, and objective student academic performance. Utilizing time-series analytics and Pearson correlation modeling, Jones demonstrated that surface-level activity metrics (such as raw page views) fail to correlate with student outcomes (r = 0.12, p > 0.05), whereas fine-grained, objective completion records and structured assessment participation metrics strongly predict overall summative achievement (r = 0.58, p < 0.01). This empirical finding substantiates CLASSREALM's design mandate to avoid superficial tracking and instead bind attendance records directly to scheduled assessment submissions and weighted grade computation.
[115 | Normal]: 2.1.3 Teacher Lesson Plan Guide Generation and Instructional Design
[116 | Normal]: Lesson planning represents a core pedagogical responsibility of Senior High School teachers, yet repetitive administrative documentation frequently detracts from instructional delivery. Integrating structured digital guides and generative pedagogical tools provides cognitive scaffolding while preserving teacher oversight.
[117 | Normal]: In a landmark local Philippine empirical investigation, Contreras and Mercado (2024) evaluated the impact of a structured Weekly Lesson Plan Monitoring Tool (WLPMT) on enhancing the instructional planning competencies of secondary school science teachers at Antonio A. Maceda Integrated School (SDO-Manila). Employing a quantitative pre-experimental repeated-measures design, teachers' lesson planning quality was tracked across four academic quarters. Empirical statistical analysis revealed that baseline lesson planning competency rose from a 1st Quarter mean of 78.10 (SD = 9.30) to 81.00 (SD = 20.41) in the 2nd Quarter, reaching 89.50 (SD = 9.10) by the 3rd Quarter. A paired-samples t-test between the 1st and 3rd quarters yielded a computed t-value of 4.45, decisively exceeding the critical two-tailed value of 2.26 (p < 0.05). The 4th Quarter maintained an elevated mean of 86.40 (SD = 12.22). This provides direct empirical proof that structured, indicator-driven lesson planning platforms significantly enhance teachers' ability to align learning competencies with classroom objectives and assessment tasks.
[118 | Normal]: Internationally, Edraga (2026) conducted a controlled experimental study published in the Journal of Digital Educational Technology investigating the direct impact of generative AI assistance on lesson plan development among N = 16 secondary education pre-service teachers (randomly assigned into n = 8 AI-assisted experimental group and n = 8 manual control group). Using standardized pedagogical rubrics evaluated by expert raters across organization, content depth, creativity, and curriculum alignment, the experimental results demonstrated that the AI-assisted group achieved significantly higher quantitative scores in structural organization (M = 4.63 vs. M = 3.75) and depth of content coverage (M = 4.50 vs. M = 3.63). Conversely, the manual group retained superior scores in localized contextual creativity (M = 4.38 vs. M = 3.88). Edraga concluded that automated lesson plan generators provide exceptional scaffolding for structural coherence and competency alignment, but must be architected as interactive teacher guides that require teacher validation and contextual customization rather than unmonitored automated text dumps.
[119 | Normal]: 2.1.4 Centralized Learning Materials Organization and Digital Repositories
[120 | Normal]: Fragmented instructional resources—spanning slide decks, PDFs, problem sets, and curriculum guides—create substantial administrative friction. Centralizing educational assets within role-governed repositories ensures equitable student access and minimizes instructional downtime.
[121 | Normal]: In the Philippines, Canlas and Ado (2024) developed and evaluated the Instructional Material Management System (IMMS) for the Polytechnic University of the Philippines (PUP). Built upon the SDLC Waterfall methodology and evaluated across N = 120 institutional stakeholders (faculty members, academic administrators, program chairs, and students) using the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality evaluation model, the system attained an overall 'Highly Acceptable' grand mean of 4.56. In the specific sub-dimensions, Security achieved a mean of 4.70, Functional Suitability scored 4.53, and Usability scored 4.45. A paired-samples t-test comparing traditional fragmented distribution (email, individual drives) with the centralized repository revealed a statistically significant reduction in file retrieval latency and material loss (p < 0.001), empirically verifying that structured digital repositories dramatically improve academic resource workflows.
[122 | Normal]: In a large-scale international empirical investigation, Januhari, Setyanto, and Kusrini (2025) evaluated e-learning platform usability and learning resource repository management across N = 384 higher education students using the ISO/IEC 25010 Quality-in-Use model and Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) in SmartPLS. The empirical findings demonstrated that platform efficiency (specifically, the speed and ease with which learners navigate and retrieve course materials) was the single strongest direct predictor of student satisfaction and continuous platform utilization (beta = 0.482, p < 0.001), accounting for 64.2% of the variance (R^2 = 0.642). The authors established that disorganized or decentralized digital file storage severely impairs student engagement, validating the necessity of categorized, role-based learning material repositories in educational software.

[131 | Normal]: 2.3 Theoretical Framework
[132 | Normal]: The theoretical foundation of this study is anchored in the updated Information Systems Success Model formulated by DeLone and McLean (2003). Recognized internationally as a benchmark theoretical paradigm for evaluating information technology implementations, the DeLone and McLean (2003) model posits that information system success is a multidimensional, interdependent construct comprising six core dimensions: System Quality, Information Quality, Service Quality, Use / Intention to Use, User Satisfaction, and Net Benefits.
[133 | Normal]: In the context of CLASSREALM's deployment at Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School for client Ma’am Karen P. Gonzaga, the DeLone and McLean (2003) model provides the causal framework explaining how technological excellence and data precision drive active adoption and produce institutional benefits:
[134 | Normal]: 1. System Quality: Evaluates the technical performance, responsiveness, and architectural reliability of CLASSREALM's hardware and software stack. This includes the sub-second card reading latency (approx. 0.10s) of the ESP32 microcontroller, non-volatile offline scan buffering during school power outages, responsive multi-platform Flutter interfaces, and PostgreSQL database reliability under Row-Level Security (RLS).
[135 | Normal]: 2. Information Quality: Measures the accuracy, timeliness, completeness, and auditability of the data outputs generated by CLASSREALM. This encompasses audit-defensible attendance logs explicitly distinguishing 'RFID' from teacher 'Manual' overrides, mathematical enforcement of 100% grading component totals, 41-tier DepEd DO 15 transmutated grades rounded to 2 decimal places, and standardized lesson plan guides.
[136 | Normal]: 3. Service Quality: Assesses the operational governance, security boundaries, and user feedback mechanisms. This includes Firebase Authentication role separation (strictly isolating Admin, Teacher, Student, and Parent roles), deterministic conflict resolution for duplicate scans and schedule cutoffs, and user-centric Inline Input Validation that eliminates cryptic database error messages.
[137 | Normal]: 4. Use / Intention to Use: Captures the daily operational engagement of stakeholders with the platform, including daily attendance scanning and grade computation by teachers, task submission and score tracking by students, and continuous academic progress monitoring by parents.
[138 | Normal]: 5. User Satisfaction: Reflects stakeholders' cognitive and practical appraisal of CLASSREALM. Driven by the 60-30-10 UI design rule, compact table rosters, persistent top header action bars, and fast edge scanning, the platform maximizes user satisfaction by eliminating repetitive clerical encoding and interface friction.
[139 | Normal]: 6. Net Benefits: Represents the tangible organizational and pedagogical gains delivered to Eduardo Barreto Sr. High School, including the complete elimination of manual roll-call delays (saving 10-15 minutes per period), eradication of DepEd grade calculation errors, proactive counseling for learners with 3 or more absences, and centralized instructional continuity.

[144 | Normal]: 2.4 Conceptual Framework
[145 | Normal]: The conceptual framework of this study is structured around the 4-part Input - Process - System - Output (IPSO) architectural paradigm. Unlike conventional three-part Input-Process-Output models that overlook the mediating technical infrastructure, the IPSO model explicitly incorporates the 'System' component, formalizing the hardware artifacts, multi-platform applications, cloud backends, and security governance layers that bind operational inputs and computational processes into verified academic deliverables.
[146 | Normal]: Figure 2
[147 | Normal]: Input-Process-System-Output (IPSO) Conceptual Framework of CLASSREALM
[148 | Normal]: 
[149 | Normal]: Figure 2 outlines the conceptual architecture of CLASSREALM, delineating the systematic flow across Input, Process, System, and Output stages. As conceptualized, raw administrative and instructional inputs are processed through specialized computational and validation algorithms within the secure CLASSREALM hardware and cloud ecosystem, producing actionable academic outputs. The four stages operate as follows:
[150 | Normal]: 1. Input: Encompasses the foundational administrative, physical, and instructional data fed into the system. This includes student demographic profiles and 12-digit LRNs from official registrar rosters, physical RFID card UIDs (captured via USB scanner or phone NFC), plotted class timetables, teacher-configured attendance cutoff thresholds (Present, Late, Absent), raw assessment scores, DepEd DO 15 component weightings, curriculum learning competencies, and instructional digital files.
[151 | Normal]: 2. Process: Represents the algorithmic logic, operational validations, and serverless workflows executed on the inputs. Key processes include schedule-bound attendance validation (blocking scans outside class hours), idempotent duplicate tap filtering, automated status evaluation, ESP32 non-volatile offline scan buffering and cloud synchronization, a nightly 23:59 Supabase Edge Function auto-marking unscanned students as Absent, real-time objective quiz checking and item analysis, DepEd DO 15 weighted grade computation and 41-tier transmutation, structured lesson plan guide generation, and role-governed resource indexing.
[152 | Normal]: 3. System (CLASSREALM): Comprises the integrated technological infrastructure and software artifacts that host and execute the processes. This includes the standalone ESP32 IoT hardware scanner (DevKit with RC522/PN532 reader, buzzer, and status LEDs), the cross-platform Flutter application layer delivering four dedicated portals (Admin Portal, Teacher Portal, Student Portal, and Parent Portal), the Supabase PostgreSQL relational database enforced by Row-Level Security (RLS), and Firebase Authentication for encrypted identity governance.
[153 | Normal]: 4. Output: Yields the verified, tangible operational and archival deliverables for institutional use. These include audit-defensible attendance logs with explicit 'RFID' or 'Manual' indicators, real-time student absence warning alerts for 3 or more absences, consolidated E-Class Record spreadsheets exportable to Excel/PDF/CSV, formatted DepEd-aligned lesson plan guides ready for classroom customization, a centralized and searchable learning materials repository, and view-only personal progress dashboards for students and parents.
